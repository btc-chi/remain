A walking guide, built from one conversation

The Root Work

Stay connected. The vine does the work.

"I am the vine. You are the branches. He who remains in me and I in him bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

John 15:5

You grew up Catholic. You did not pay attention in Sunday school. A few weeks ago that changed, and now it is personal. This guide holds everything from our study in one place, so you can read it, act on it, and build from it.

Start here

Three things before anything else.

One warning, because it protects the rest

Jesus described people a few weeks into faith. Some seed lands on rock. Those people receive the word with joy. They believe for a while. Then a test comes, and they fall away. He gives the reason: they have no root.

"Those on the rock are they who, when they hear, receive the word with joy; but these have no root, who believe for a while, then fall away in time of temptation."

Luke 8:13

Your joy now is real. Joy alone does not hold you in a storm. Roots hold you. Roots are daily Scripture, prayer, a church, and obedience. Everything below is root work.

Test what you are told

"...they received the word with all readiness of mind, examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so."

Acts 17:11

That includes this guide. Check every claim against the text.

About the translation

Scripture here is the World English Bible, which is public domain. You quote the NIV, so the wording will differ slightly in places. The meaning does not. Every passage carries its reference so you can pull the translation you prefer.

Each scripture block in this file also carries a data-ref attribute with its reference. If you build the app, you can parse this file and link every verse straight to your Bible API.

Your verses, corrected

You said you botched them. You will lean on these, so they have to be exact.

1. Romans 8:28 — not "always doing good work"

"We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, for those who are called according to his purpose."

Romans 8:28

The verse does not say all things are good. It says God works inside all things, even bad ones, toward good. Read the next verse. It defines the good: you become like his Son.

2. Galatians 5:22-23 — not Corinthians

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law."

Galatians 5:22-23

That is nine. You missed joy, peace, patience, and kindness. Note that "fruit" is singular. It is one fruit with nine parts. You do not pick the parts you like. You probably mixed this with 1 Corinthians 13, the love chapter.

3. John 15:5 — you merged two verses

The vine verse is at the top of this guide. "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord" is a different text. It is Psalm 118:26, the crowd's shout at the entry into Jerusalem. You joined the vine and the parade.

4. Romans 12:1-2 — you nearly had it

"Therefore I urge you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your spiritual service. Don't be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, well-pleasing, and perfect will of God."

Romans 12:1-2

Add verse 1. The surrender comes first. The renewed mind comes after it. Note the word "living." A dead sacrifice stays on the altar. A living one can climb off. So this is a daily choice.

5. The troubled heart — four moments, not one

"Don't let your heart be troubled. Believe in God. Believe also in me."

John 14:1 — the Last Supper

"Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, give I to you. Don't let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful."

John 14:27 — the Last Supper

"In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world."

John 16:33 — end of his last teaching

"Peace be to you."

John 20:19 — after the resurrection

The pattern. In John 12:27 his soul is troubled. In 13:21 he is troubled again. In 14:1 he tells his friends not to be troubled. He takes the trouble and gives them the peace. In 20:19-20 he says "Peace be to you" and then shows them his hands and his side. The scars are the price of your peace.

6. John 14:6 — you had this one exact

"I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me."

John 14:6

Also from the Last Supper, the same night as "do not let your heart be troubled." It is his answer to Thomas, who asked how they could know the way.

7 and 8. Psalm 139 — both of yours are in one chapter

"If I ascend up into heaven, you are there. If I make my bed in Sheol, behold, you are there!"

Psalm 139:8

"I will give thanks to you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Your works are wonderful. My soul knows that very well."

Psalm 139:14

Sheol means the grave. No place is outside his reach, not even death. Read the whole psalm. He knows you (1-6). He is everywhere (7-12). He made you (13-16). It closes with a prayer: search me, God, and know my heart (23-24).

9. Psalm 118:22 — the cornerstone

"The stone which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner."

Psalm 118:22

This comes from the same psalm as the shout at the entry. Jesus quotes it in the Temple a few days later (Luke 20:17). Peter quotes it in Acts 4:11. The stones that could cry out, the stones that fall in AD 70, and the cornerstone all sit in the same week.

10. Giving yourself to him

"If anyone desires to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me."

Luke 9:23

The phrase you reached for about glory is John 17:1-2. Jesus prays, "Father, the hour has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you," and then speaks of giving eternal life to all whom the Father has given him.

"Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who believe in me through their word."

John 17:20

That is you. He prayed for you in that room, before the cross. You give yourself to him. The Father already gave you to him.

Who he is

You said he was a man, and that this makes him relatable. True, and half the truth.

The man

"For we don't have a high priest who can't be touched with the feeling of our infirmities, but one who has been in all points tempted like we are, yet without sin."

Hebrews 4:15

He suffered when he was tempted, so he can help you when you are tempted (Hebrews 2:18).

The God

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God... The Word became flesh, and lived among us."

John 1:1, 14

He was in the form of God. He took the form of a servant and obeyed to the point of a cross (Philippians 2:6-8).

Both matter. A man alone cannot save you. A God who never became a man cannot fully understand you. He is both. Hebrews 4:16 gives you the result: come to his throne with confidence.

The church stated this at the Council of Chalcedon, AD 451: fully God and fully man, in one person.

c. AD 27-29

Immediately after

Luke 9:51

c. AD 30 or 33

Forty days

Watch the bookends. His first recorded words, age twelve: "Didn't you know that I must be in my Father's house?" (Luke 2:49). His last words on the cross: "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit" (Luke 23:46). His whole life sits between those two sentences.

For your family. His brother James did not believe in him. After the resurrection, Jesus appeared to James (1 Corinthians 15:7). James became a leader of the Jerusalem church and wrote the book that carries his name. A doubting brother became a pillar. Keep praying for yours.

Why he obeyed when it was hard

You asked this directly. The text gives five answers.

  1. Love. "The world must know that I love the Father" (John 14:31). Obedience was how he showed it. He asks the same of you: "If you love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15).
  2. It fed him. "My food is to do the will of him who sent me" (John 4:34). Obedience gave him strength. It did not only cost him strength.
  3. Trust. He did only what he saw the Father doing (John 5:19). He never walked alone.
  4. The joy ahead. He looked past the pain to the result. You are part of that result.
  5. Honest prayer. Hebrews 5:7 says he prayed with loud cries and tears. It did not come easily. He wrestled, then said "not my will, but yours."

"...who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising its shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."

Hebrews 12:2

Then Hebrews 5:8 adds the line you should sit with: he learned obedience from what he suffered. Jesus learned obedience through hardship. You will too.

The twelve

Ordinary men. Broken in public. Used anyway.

Peter, Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, Judas Iscariot. Luke names "Judas son of James" where Mark has Thaddaeus. Most scholars say they are the same man.

Their troubles

  • Matthew collected taxes for Rome. Simon was a Zealot, a rebel against Rome. Jesus put both men on one team.
  • James and John wanted to call fire down on a village (Luke 9:54). He nicknamed them Sons of Thunder. They also asked for the best seats.
  • They argued about who was greatest, even at the Last Supper (Luke 22:24).
  • Thomas refused to believe without proof. Eight days later he said "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). That is one of the clearest statements in the New Testament that Jesus is God.
  • Peter walked on water, then sank. He confessed Jesus as Messiah, and a few verses later Jesus called him Satan. He swore he would die with Jesus, then denied him three times.
  • All of them fled (Mark 14:50). That is Zechariah 13:7 coming true. The shepherd was struck and the sheep scattered.

Then the turn

In John 21:15-19 Jesus asks Peter three times, "Do you love me?" Three denials, three restorations, then "Follow me." About fifty days after the denial, Peter preached in Jerusalem and three thousand believed.

"Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and had perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marveled. They recognized that they had been with Jesus."

Acts 4:13

That answers your worry about Sunday school. Peter had no religious training at all. Time with Jesus was the difference. That is what you are doing now.

Paul

He persecuted the church, then met Jesus on the road (Acts 9). In 2 Corinthians 11:24-27 he lists his trials: five whippings of thirty-nine lashes, three beatings with rods, one stoning, three shipwrecks. He wrote "Rejoice in the Lord always" from prison. In 1 Timothy 1:15 he calls himself the worst of sinners.

"But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."

Romans 5:8

You said you are a sinner and he still wanted you. That is the verse for it.

How the Bible came together

It is a library, not one book.

Why your Catholic Bible has more books

Protestant Bibles have 66 books: 39 Old Testament, 27 New. Catholic Bibles have 73. The extra seven are Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach, and Baruch, plus additions to Esther and Daniel. Catholics call them deuterocanonical. Protestants call them the Apocrypha. The Jewish Bible does not include them. The Council of Trent fixed the Catholic list in 1546.

You do not need to settle this now. Every book in this guide is on both lists.

The Old Testament

Israel collected it over centuries. The Hebrew Bible has three parts: the Law, the Prophets, and the Writings. Jesus names that shape himself in Luke 24:44. In 1947 shepherds found the Dead Sea Scrolls, including a complete Isaiah scroll from about 125 BC. It is a thousand years older than the oldest copy known before it, and it matches closely.

The New Testament

  • Paul's letters came first, roughly AD 48-65.
  • 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 preserves a creed about the death, burial, resurrection, and appearances. Most scholars, including skeptics, date it to within a few years of the cross.
  • Mark was likely first, about AD 60-70. Matthew and Luke followed. John came last, about AD 90.
  • Luke states his method: he investigated carefully from eyewitnesses and wrote an orderly account (Luke 1:1-4).
  • The church did not invent the list. It recognized books already in use. The tests were apostolic link, agreement with the faith, and use across the churches.
  • Athanasius listed our exact 27 books in AD 367. Councils at Hippo (393) and Carthage (397) confirmed it.
  • More than 5,800 Greek manuscripts survive. No other ancient book comes close.

The whole story in ten steps

Every book sits somewhere on this line.

  1. Creation. God makes a good world. Genesis 1-2
  2. The fall. Humans rebel and death enters. Genesis 3:15 holds the first promise of a rescuer.
  3. The promise. God chooses Abraham to bless all nations. Genesis 12:1-3
  4. The rescue. God frees Israel from Egypt and gives the Law. The Passover lamb appears here. Exodus
  5. The kingdom. God promises David a throne that lasts forever. 2 Samuel 7:12-16
  6. The exile. Israel breaks the covenant. Babylon destroys Jerusalem in 586 BC. The prophets promise a new covenant. Jeremiah 31:31-34
  7. The silence. About four hundred years between Malachi and John the Baptist.
  8. The King. Jesus lives, dies, and rises. the Gospels
  9. The church. The Spirit comes and the news spreads. Acts and the letters
  10. The new creation. God lives with his people. Revelation 21:3-4

The man who wept over Jerusalem is the one who wipes away every tear.

How to pray

The disciples asked your exact question: "Lord, teach us to pray." Luke 11:1.

He answered with the Our Father. You know it by heart from your Catholic years. Now pray it slowly, one line at a time, and mean each line.

Our Father
You speak to your Father, not a stranger. The Spirit lets you call him Abba (Romans 8:15).
Hallowed be your name
Start with worship, not requests.
Your will be done
Put his plans above yours. This is Gethsemane in small form.
Daily bread
Ask for today's needs. "Daily" means you come back tomorrow.
Forgive us, as we forgive
Confess, and release grudges. He explains this in Matthew 6:14-15.
Deliver us
Ask for protection.

The Holy Spirit

A correction first, because it will save you a lot of confusion.

You said you want to feel the Holy Spirit. Scripture does not measure him by feelings. It measures him by fruit and direction. Feelings rise and fall. Fruit lasts.

"The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don't know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit."

John 3:8

He said that to Nicodemus, the man he told "you must be born again." Nicodemus was also a secret believer, afraid of what people would say. At the burial he came out in the open with seventy-five pounds of spices (John 19:39). You are somewhere in his story.

What the Spirit actually does

  • Teaches you and reminds you of Jesus's words. John 14:26
  • Shows you your sin. John 16:8
  • Guides you into truth. John 16:13
  • Confirms inside you that you are God's child. Romans 8:16
  • Helps you pray when you have no words. Romans 8:26
  • Grows the fruit. Galatians 5:22
  • Can be grieved by your sin. Ephesians 4:30

"The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit that we are children of God."

That is the inner certainty you described. In practice, keeping in step with the Spirit (Galatians 5:25) means this: obey the small prompt now, confess sin quickly, and stay in the Word he inspired.

On "since I was a boy." Jesus said no one comes to him unless the Father draws him (John 6:44). What you felt as a boy may have been that drawing. Catholic teaching ties the Spirit to baptism. Many evangelicals tie his indwelling to conversion (Ephesians 1:13). The traditions disagree. Both agree God pursued you long before you answered.

The prophecy, about 500 years earlier

"Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion! Shout, daughter of Jerusalem! Behold, your King comes to you! He is righteous, and having salvation; lowly, and riding on a donkey, even on a colt, the foal of a donkey."

Zechariah 9:9

Hebrew poetry says each thing twice. Daughter of Zion and daughter of Jerusalem are one city. The donkey and the colt are one animal. Read the eight verses before it: God marches through the region like a conqueror. Then the king arrives on a donkey, not a war horse. Deuteronomy 17:16 told Israel's king not to collect horses. The horse was the animal of war.

The day it happened

He arranged it on purpose. He sent two disciples for a colt nobody had ridden (Luke 19:30). The crowd shouted "Hosanna," which means "save us, please" (Psalm 118:25). They wanted a king who would drive out Rome.

Then he wept

"When he came near, he saw the city and wept over it, saying, 'If you, even you, had known today the things which belong to your peace! But now, they are hidden from your eyes. For the days will come on you, when your enemies will throw up a barricade against you, surround you, hem you in on every side, and will dash you and your children within you to the ground. They will not leave in you one stone on another, because you didn't know the time of your visitation.'"

Luke 19:41-44
  • The Greek word for "wept" here means loud wailing, not quiet tears. At Lazarus's grave a different, quieter word is used.
  • Three verses earlier he said that if the crowd went silent, the stones would cry out. Now he says no stone will be left on another. The same stones, twice.
  • "The time of your visitation" points back to Luke 1:68 and 1:78, where God visits his people. God came in person, and the city did not recognize him.
  • Rome destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70, about forty years later. Titus built a wall around the entire city, which is what verse 43 describes.

The misalignment you spotted. They were right to celebrate. They had the wrong kind of king in mind. They wanted a throne. He came for a cross. Zechariah 9:11 already joined that king to "the blood of the covenant," and at the supper he said "this is my blood of the new covenant." He did save them. He saved them by body and blood, not by driving Rome out of Zion.

The same week, in John's account

  • Mary pours a year's wages of perfume on his feet the night before. He says she kept it for his burial. She understood his death before anyone else. John 12:1-8
  • Judas objects, because he was stealing from the money bag. John 12:6
  • Greeks ask to see him, and he says "the hour has come." Gentiles arriving is the signal. John 12:20-23

"Most certainly I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit."

John 12:24

"Now my soul is troubled. What shall I say? 'Father, save me from this time?' But I came to this time for this cause."

John 12:27

He asks for escape, then refuses it in the same breath. Then he prays "Father, glorify your name." The grief was real and the choice was firm. Both at once.

Why the crowd could not see it

John stops the story and explains. He quotes Isaiah 53:1 and Isaiah 6:10. Then he says something enormous: Isaiah "saw his glory and spoke of him" (John 12:41). In Isaiah 6, Isaiah sees the Lord on a throne, high and lifted up. Isaiah 52:13 uses those same two words for the Servant who suffers. The throne and the cross belong to the same person. That is why Jesus said "when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself."

The method, so you can do this yourself

  1. Read the whole chapter before the verse.
  2. In poetry, look for the pair. The second line usually repeats the first.
  3. Check where the New Testament quotes the Old. A study Bible lists these in the margin.
  4. Compare the four Gospels side by side. Each one keeps details the others drop.
  5. Ask what the passage shows about God, and what it asks of you.
